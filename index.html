Where the data comes from

None of it is real. Your browser makes up 500 B2B leads every time the page loads, shaped like a mid-market pipeline across Australia, the Netherlands, India, the UK, Singapore and New Zealand. Each lead has a company, a job title, a lead source and a history of activity: email clicks, page visits, webinars, event chats, pricing views and demo requests.

Each lead also has a hidden answer to "would sales take this?", based on how ready they are to buy and how well they fit. The score never sees that answer, the same way your marketing automation never hears what sales hears on the first call.

How marketers can use it

  • Try a scoring change here before you make it in HubSpot, Marketo or Dynamics 365 Customer Insights, and see what it does to MQL volume and sales acceptance.
  • Open it in a sales and marketing meeting and agree on the MQL threshold together, with both teams watching the trade-off between more leads and better leads.
  • Use the missed buyers to make the case for SDR follow-up or a nurture track for leads sitting just under the line.
  • Check your channels. If one source keeps sending MQLs that sales turns down, give fewer points to what that channel creates.
  • To use your own data, export leads with their activity and the sales outcome from your CRM and swap them in. The README explains how.

1. Pick a scoring style

A scoring style decides which signals count as interest. Some teams reward who the lead is, others reward what the lead did. Each style gives a different list to sales.

Leads at or above this score go to sales. Lower it and sales gets more leads, but more of them won't be ready. Raise it and sales gets fewer, better leads, but some real buyers wait in nurture.

How scoring works

Score = fit + engagement

Fit is who the lead is: role seniority, company size, and whether they're in a target industry (financial services, healthcare, government, energy & utilities). Engagement is what they did, like requesting a demo, viewing pricing or attending a webinar.

Old activity fades

An event loses half its points every half-life (30 days by default). Only the latest three of each event type count, ten for page visits, so clicking forty emails can't carry a lead. Unsubscribing takes points off in full.

When a lead becomes an MQL

Its score reaches the threshold and it has done something in the last 90 days. A perfect-fit account that has gone quiet stays in nurture.

Where SQLs come from

Each sample lead has a hidden flag for whether sales would accept it, based on real buying intent and fit. The score can only guess intent from behaviour, so the MQL → SQL rate shows how well your weights read it.
