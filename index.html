Lead scoring from first principles
1

Sales time is the scarce thing

Reps spend only about 30% of their week selling (Salesforce, State of Sales). A Journal of Marketing study found about 70% of marketing leads were never followed up. Scoring decides who gets those few hours.

2

A score is a bet

It estimates how likely a lead is to buy soon. Forrester puts the share of leads that become customers below 1%, so most of your list should not go to sales.

3

Fit says could, behaviour says now

Role, size and industry tell you if they could buy. What they did lately tells you if they are buying now. Only about 5% of B2B buyers are in market at any time (Ehrenberg-Bass for the LinkedIn B2B Institute).

4

Signals differ, and they fade

A pricing visit last week beats a download last year. This lab ignores email opens, because Apple's Mail Privacy Protection (2021) made them unreliable.

5

The line is a trade

Move it right and sales trusts you more, but you miss buyers. Move it left and you catch more buyers, but waste calls. Only better weights improve both.

6

AI learns the weights

Predictive scoring does the same maths but learns the weights from your won and lost deals. Dynamics 365 Sales needs at least 40 qualified and 40 disqualified leads to train a model (Microsoft Learn).

Where the data comes from, and sources

None of the leads are real. Your browser makes up 500 B2B leads shaped like a mid-market pipeline across Australia, the Netherlands, India, the UK, Singapore and New Zealand. Each one has a company, a job title, a source and a history of activity.

Each lead also carries a hidden answer to "would sales take this?", based on how ready they are to buy and how well they fit. The score never sees it, just as your marketing automation never hears the first sales call. That's what makes it a fair test.

Use it to try a scoring change before you make it in HubSpot, Marketo or Dynamics 365, or open it in a sales and marketing meeting and agree on the MQL line together.
