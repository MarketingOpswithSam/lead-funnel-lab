How scoring works

score = fit points + engagement points fit = seniority share × seniority weight + size share × size weight + industry share × industry weight event = weight × 0.5 ^ (days ago ÷ half-life) MQL = score ≥ threshold, and active in the last 90 days SQL = MQL that the simulated sales team accepts

Fit: who they are

Each fit slider sets the points for a perfect match. A C-level contact gets the full seniority points, a director 70%, an individual contributor 15%. Companies with 1,000 to 4,999 staff get full size points. Financial services, healthcare, government and energy & utilities count as target industries; everything else gets 30%.

Engagement: what they did

Every event earns its slider's points, then fades with age. An event one half-life old counts half, two half-lives old a quarter. Only the three most recent events of each type count (ten for page views), so a lead that clicks forty emails can't run away with the score. An unsubscribe takes its penalty in full.

When a lead becomes an MQL

The total has to reach the threshold and the lead has to have done something in the last 90 days. A perfect-fit account that has gone quiet stays in nurture.

Where SQLs come from

Every sample lead carries a hidden flag for whether sales would accept it, driven by the lead's real buying intent and fit. The model can only guess intent from behaviour. The sliders decide who you hand over, and the hidden flag decides who sales keeps, so the MQL → SQL rate shows how well your weights read intent.
