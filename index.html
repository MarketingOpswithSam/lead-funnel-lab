Six rules of lead scoring

Tap "Try it" and watch the dots
1

Sales time is the scarce thing

Reps spend only about 30% of their week selling (Salesforce, State of Sales). Research in the Journal of Marketing reports that as many as 70% of marketing leads never get followed up by sales. Scoring decides who gets those few hours.

2

A score is a bet

It estimates how likely a lead is to buy soon. Forrester puts the share of leads that become customers below 1%, so most of your list should not go to sales, and only some leads sales accepts ever close.

3

Fit says could, behaviour says now

Role, size and industry tell you if they could buy. What they did lately tells you if they are buying now. Only about 5% of B2B buyers are in market at any time (Ehrenberg-Bass for the LinkedIn B2B Institute).

4

Signals differ, and they fade

A pricing visit last week beats a download last year. This lab ignores email opens, because Apple's Mail Privacy Protection (2021) made them unreliable.

5

The line is a trade

Move it right and sales trusts you more, but you miss buyers. Move it left and you catch more buyers, but waste calls. Only better weights improve both.

6

AI learns the weights

Predictive scoring takes the same idea but learns from your won and lost deals instead of weights you set. Dynamics 365 Sales needs at least 40 qualified and 40 disqualified leads to train a model (Microsoft Learn).
